How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Long waits for hot water at sinks far from the heater
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • Rising gas or electric bills with no change in business volume
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Hot water running out during your busiest hour

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A commercial heater is not just a large residential one. Higher gas input means the supply line, meter, combustion air, and vent all have to be sized for the load. Heavy electric units can strain a panel that was never meant to carry them. Storage temperatures of 140 degrees or more help with sanitation but create scald risk, so properly sized mixing valves are essential. And a recirculation system only works when the pump, check valves, and balancing valves are all doing their part; if not, the far end of the building waits while the near end runs hot.

Code compliance and scheduling matter just as much. Permits and inspections are standard for commercial work, larger tanks may carry extra requirements, and any delay at inspection can keep a kitchen or laundromat closed. Antioch Water Heater handles that coordination up front, plans installations for early mornings, evenings, or closed days, and replaces banked units one at a time whenever the layout allows. The result is a system built for your busiest hour, installed correctly, and brought online with as little disruption to your customers as possible.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
