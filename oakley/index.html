How Oakley Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Oakley

Whatever type of water heater your Oakley home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
